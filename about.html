Background

Spencer earned his M.A. in Applied Clinical Psychology from Pacific University in January 2026. He completed his undergraduate degree in psychology with a minor in kinesiology at Pacific in 2023.

He has coached skiing, mountain biking, and rowing and worked as a bike mechanic, ski technician, ski patroller, and outdoor guide. Outside of work, he’s usually outdoors or in his workshop.

Research Experience

Exercise prescription in behavioral health

While reviewing exercise interventions, Spencer noticed that studies sometimes named the activity without describing its intensity, duration, or delivery in enough detail to compare programs built around the same activity. He developed the Seven Specifier Framework for Exercise Prescription to describe what participants are asked to do and how exercise is incorporated into treatment. He presented the framework at the Society of Behavioral Medicine’s 2026 Annual Meeting and at Pacific University Research Day. He built Exercise Px to demonstrate how the framework can be used and invite feedback.

Social media and disordered eating

For his master’s thesis, supervised by Dr. Marshall Beauchamp, Spencer examined social media use and disordered eating in 100 adults. He developed an 84-item survey in Qualtrics to accompany the Eating Disorder Examination Questionnaire, recruited participants through Prolific, and conducted reliability and regression analyses. Comparisons based on time spent online did not reach statistical significance, while specific appearance-related beliefs and behaviors were associated with symptoms. Because the study was cross-sectional, it could not establish which came first. He is revising the manuscript for journal resubmission.

Rural culture and mental health

In an independent study with Dr. Lisa Christiansen, Spencer reviewed research on rural mental health and developed teaching materials for a Cultural Foundations course. The project led to Roots and Realities, a conference poster introducing three lenses clinicians can use to understand a client’s experience of rural life.

Research Interests

Spencer wants to test how changing individual specifiers in an exercise prescription affects psychological outcomes. He is particularly interested in identifying physiological responses that can be deliberately targeted through exercise. One question he wants to pursue is whether designing a session to elicit a particular physiological response can improve a specific outcome, such as attention or emotion regulation, and what exercise dose is needed to elicit that response.
